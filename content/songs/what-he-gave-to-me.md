"A friend of mine told me about her dad, when she was 6. The light in her eyes, and in her soul. Spoke this song."

She'd climb onto his lap most every day

Ask him please to tickle her and play

He'd drink the coffee that she made

Though it must have tasted terrible, they say

He'd stop before he'd leave and wait

For the kiss she had to give him at the gate

He built a swing out in the yard

Pushed her high, but not too hard

Brought home injured birds and strays

Knowing what would heal her heart always

Taught her gentle strength in little ways

In the ordinary moments, ordinary days

You can paint the house a different shade

You can pull the hedge, let the old things fade

You can take the swing down from the tree

But you can't take what he gave to me

'Cause wisdom doesn't hang on nails and rope

Wisdom lives in how we love and hope

Long after wood and metal rust away

What a father leaves, in his daughter stays

Now she tends to wounded things that fly

Talks to plants and asks them why

Makes sure the geckos get their light

Draws the hurt from souls in healing rite

Feels the pain folks hide inside

Loves with that same gentle pride

They tore the swing down years ago

But she still holds what she needs to know

About a good man, how he shows

His love in small ways as she grows

The coffee wasn't coffee, see?

It was him saying "you matter to me"

The kiss wasn't just goodbye

It was "I see you" in reply

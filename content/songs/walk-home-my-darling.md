## Wayist Insight

This is a bedside song. A spouse singing to a beloved who is dying. It is not a performance — it is a blessing spoken into the ear of someone crossing the threshold. And everything in it rests on a single Wayist understanding that changes the entire experience of death: you cannot destroy energy. You can only change it.

The body is a material container. When it fails, what lived inside it — the soul, a subtle energy body — is not destroyed. It cannot be. Energy does not end; it transforms. This is not metaphor or comfort language. It is the Wayist understanding of what we are: energy beings temporarily housed in matter for the purpose of learning. When the house is done, the being walks on.

The song knows this, and because it knows this, its grief is real but its terror is gone. "This isn't goodbye, it's see you soon" is not denial. It is the calm recognition that the beloved is going somewhere — not into nothing, but through the psychomesion (the threshold between material and spiritual realms) into Paradise. And their Tara, the graduated soul who has walked beside them through every lifetime, is the one who walks them through.

**Paradise as waystation.** The song says "until we walk together again on the Butterfly Path in Paradise." In Wayist understanding, Paradise (puristhana) is not a final destination — it is a temporary waystation where souls process between incarnations. Freed from the material body's sense of time, the soul can replay and examine the life just lived, extracting the wisdom and growth it was meant to gather. When that review is complete, the law of karma determines when and how the soul returns for the next semester of learning. Paradise is the between-place, a chrysalis between incarnations. It is also where souls are prepared to join the school of the Butterfly Path. The final destination — Sukhāvatī, the realm governed by our Father and Mother God — is where souls go after graduation, when the Butterfly Path is complete.

**The Tara at the threshold.** "I can see Tara here beside you / Their light is filling up this room." The song describes what many experience at the moment of death — a presence, a light, a sense of being met. In Wayist teaching, this is not hallucination or fading brain chemistry. Your Tara has walked with you through every life. At the moment of transition, they become visible — or at least felt — as they take your hand and guide you across.

This echoes the oldest teaching in the catalogue. When Lord Jesus spoke from the cross to Mari, his final instruction was: *Come to the Mother.* Ehi elaya — come. In sacred darkness, the Mother awaits. The dying often sense this feminine divine presence — the Mother principle, whether experienced as Tara, as Mother God, or simply as a warm darkness that receives rather than rejects. The song's bridge — Tara's light filling the room, healing every wound, taking the hand so gently — is the same moment the Poem from the Cross describes in Sanskrit.

**The freedom that changes how we live.** There is a profound psychological difference between people who fear death and those who embrace it as part of life. When death is annihilation, every moment is shadowed by dread. When death is a doorway — a walking-home guided by a companion who knows the way — then life itself opens up. You stop clinging to the material container and start seeing life as what it is: energy, everywhere, in everything, not confined to the forms we recognise but flowing through all of them. The storms and sunlit mornings, the joys and sorrows — they were never random suffering. They were "part of the sacred ride," curriculum in a school the soul chose to attend.

The song's last sound is breathing, then peace. Not silence as absence — peace as arrival.

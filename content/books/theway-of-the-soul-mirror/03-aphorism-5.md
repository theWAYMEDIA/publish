#### “The way to tranquil minds and inner success is accepting whatever situation you find yourself; be grateful for all you have to work with, to improve things; adapting to conditions; seeking nothing more than to distil from the experience whatever wisdom is needed to become an immortal spiritual being.

#### This will unite you with theWAY and bring ultimate prosperity.”

 

Live your life intent on contributing to the welfare of all creatures. When you use a tree, plant a tree. We respect the cycles of life and see ourselves part of it

Gratitude is the great healer and an excellent teacher. The energy of gratitude in our being heals at many levels of the self.

Always be grateful for that which you have to work with, the ability to improve things, and that will lead you forward into the good energies that bring prosperity of soul and spirit. To be sad over a loss is a good sign of attachment and that too is a lesson; the sadness will pass. In the meantime, count your blessings and know that you have a storehouse of good fortune to lead you on as you continue on theWAY.

This aphorism is understood in the greater Wayist ethic that is fundamental to our philosophy: “… work to change what is unjust, and if you cannot, move elsewhere”. From experience, over millennia, we know that laws and rulers are not always just and that low-level souls sometimes prey on good people. Therefore, we work to restore justice, safety, and peace, but if it cannot be done, we leave to live elsewhere.

To live in a wholesome energy community and maintain one’s inner beauty is of ultimate importance. The discomfort and cost of relocating is a temporary setback, but the prize of being at peace is of enduring benefit.

Should a crime or injustice be committed against you, act quickly to find the simplest and safest path to move on with life and restore your peace. Nothing on Earth is important enough to sacrifice your inner energies for, and to become toxic from-inside-out.

Like water and air, so are community energies. When the water or air in the community is poisonous, and you cannot quickly fix it, move away already, start over elsewhere and have a wholesome life.

 

#### The Challenge of Our Time

We who practice theWAY face a unique challenge in this age. For millennia, human beings developed exquisite senses to distinguish wholesome food from poison, medicine from toxin. Our ancestors could taste, smell, and feel their way to health and vitality. This was sacred knowledge, passed down through generations like a spiritual inheritance.

In barely two generations, this ancient wisdom has become nearly useless. The very building blocks of life — our food, water, and air — have been fundamentally altered by forces our senses cannot detect. We find ourselves like children in a new world, lacking the discernment our great-grandparents took for granted.

An elder once told me: “When I was young, bread lasted two days before it spoiled. Milk would sour and become cottage cheese. Now my grandchildren eat bread that sits unchanged for weeks, and milk that turns to toxic sludge. Even the insects won’t touch what we call food.” This is not progress — it is a breaking of the sacred covenant between human and Earth.

Consider this sobering reality: recent studies found synthetic materials circulating in human blood — substances that never existed in nature now flow through our bodies and cross into unborn children. We have become unwitting participants in a vast experiment, one that would horrify our ancestors who understood the body as a temple.

The food industry has replaced nature’s pharmacy with a chemical laboratory. Where once we could trust our senses to guide us toward nourishment, we now must become detectives, reading labels filled with unpronounceable compounds. Bread contains dozens of synthetic “conditioners,” fruits are bred for appearance over nutrition, and even organic foods may carry residues our bodies cannot process.

This is not merely about health — it is about spiritual integrity. When we poison our vessel, we cloud our connection to Heaven. When we participate unknowingly in the poisoning of Earth, we accumulate karmic debt. The biome within us — one of our ten minds — is being systematically destroyed, leaving us spiritually and physically malnourished despite apparent abundance.

#### The Wayist Response

Yet despair is not theWAY. As always, we adapt and find wisdom in difficulty. This challenge calls forth new skills, deeper discernment, and greater spiritual maturity

First, we must reclaim the ancient art of sensing wholesome food. If our great-grandmothers were transported to our time, they would recoil from much of what we call nourishment. We must redevelop this sensitivity through mindful eating —  asking our bodies how they feel after different foods, paying attention to energy levels, mood, and clarity of thought.

Second, we vote with our spending. Every purchase is a spiritual choice. When we buy real food — food that spoils as nature intended, food grown in harmony with Earth’s cycles — we create demand for righteousness. Corporations follow money; if we collectively choose life over convenience, the market will respond.

Third, we embrace simplicity. Our ancestors thrived on rice and vegetables, seasonal fruits, and modest portions of clean meat. They built cities and raised large families on diets our modern dietitians would call “inadequate.” Perhaps the problem lies not in their simplicity, but in our complexity.

Fourth, we remember that we are guests on this Earth. The Wayist asks: “When I use a tree, do I plant a tree?” This principle extends to all consumption. We seek to give back more than we take, to leave the Earth more wholesome for our presence.

Finally, we hold our leaders accountable with the gravity this situation deserves. This is not a political issue — it is a matter of survival for all living beings. Those who profit from poisoning the commons must face the consequences of their actions, just as we would remove any predator that threatened our community’s children.

#### The Path Forward

The solution lies not in fear, but in returning to wisdom. We must become sophisticated in our simplicity — choosing fewer, better things over cheap abundance. We must support those who grow real food and prepare it with love. We must teach our children to taste the difference between what nourishes and what merely fills.

Most importantly, we must remember that this challenge, like all challenges, serves our spiritual development. Learning to navigate a toxic world without becoming toxic ourselves is advanced soul-craft. Maintaining compassion for those who profit from harm while protecting ourselves and our loved ones requires the highest spiritual skills.

The same forces that created this crisis can heal it, if we direct them wisely. We are not victims — we are powerful spiritual beings having a human experience. The market responds to our choices, governments respond to our demands, and the Earth responds to our care.

As the old sage reminds us: “A person who steals from his mother, who beats his mother, who pours poison on her, who robs her garden, who steals from her children, and never replaces what was taken —  that person disrespects Mother Earth. She supplies the building material to create all that we know, and all that we need.”

We can choose differently. We can be the generation that turns the tide, that remembers what our bodies truly need, that honors the sacred relationship between human and Earth. This is not just about survival — it is about thriving in alignment with theWAY.

When we eat consciously, buy wisely, and live simply, we participate in the healing of the world. This is our practice, our service, our path to Heaven on Earth.
